\chapter{Study Area and Data}

This chapter describes the study area, the four groups of data (ground \pmtf{}, satellite,
reanalysis and static features) and what an exploratory analysis of them shows. All data were
retrieved programmatically except the CPCB hourly files, which had to be downloaded through the
portal's web interface. Every raw file is listed with its source, date range, size and SHA-256
hash in a data manifest of 3,081 files, which is checked against the disk before each run.

\section{Study area and period}

The study area comprises Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana
(Figure~\ref{fig:study_area}). Data were extracted for 1 December 2020 to 31 December 2025 (IST);
the analysis period is 2021--2025, and the extra month supplies lagged features for early January
2021. Seasons follow the India Meteorological Department convention: winter (January--February),
pre-monsoon (March--May), south-west monsoon (June--September) and north-east monsoon
(October--December).

\section{Ground-level \texorpdfstring{\pmtf{}}{PM2.5}}

Hourly \pmtf{} came from two sources (Table~\ref{tab:ground}). The main source is the CPCB CCR
repository \cite{cpcbccr}: 833 station-year files, downloaded by hand because the portal requires a
CAPTCHA. The US Consulate monitors in Hyderabad and Chennai are not part of the CPCB network and
were taken from OpenAQ \cite{openaq}. Low-cost sensors were excluded. A CPCB station and an OpenAQ
location were treated as the same site if their names matched or they were within 500\,m of each
other, in which case the CPCB record was kept.

Two problems in the raw data needed attention before any analysis. First, the time convention of
the CPCB labels is not documented. Aligning CPCB with OpenAQ's explicit UTC timestamps at five
CPCB stations whose records are also mirrored on OpenAQ (one per state), over two weeks of 2025, gave the best match when CPCB labels are read as the start of the
hour in IST (median correlation 0.986, against 0.944 for hour-end labels), so that convention is
used throughout. Second, until October 2024 OpenAQ returned most US Consulate hours twice, as
one-hour bins starting at :00 and at :30 IST. One record per IST hour is kept, which removed 30,111
duplicates (Table~\ref{tab:ground}).

\section{Satellite data}

Two satellite products were used, both extracted with Google Earth Engine \cite{gorelick2017gee}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{TROPOMI} (Sentinel-5P, offline L3): tropospheric NO\textsubscript{2}, CO,
        SO\textsubscript{2}, HCHO, the UV aerosol index and cloud fraction
        \cite{veefkind2012tropomi}. The Earth Engine L3 product is already filtered on each
        retrieval's quality value.
  \item \textbf{MAIAC} (MODIS, 1\,km daily): AOD at 550\,nm \cite{lyapustin2018maiac}, using
        retrievals flagged as clear.
\end{itemize}

Each variable was averaged over each IST day at the station pixel and within 5\,km and 10\,km of
the station, together with the number of valid pixels. The 10\,km buffer is used in the models
because it keeps far more monsoon days (Table~\ref{tab:sat_avail}).

\section{Reanalysis and static data}

Hourly 2\,m temperature, dew point, 10\,m wind and precipitation come from ERA5-Land
\cite{munozsabater2021era5land}. ERA5-Land has no land cell at nine coastal stations, where ERA5
\cite{hersbach2020era5} is used instead; ERA5 also supplies boundary-layer height. Relative humidity
is computed from temperature and dew point. MERRA-2 \cite{gelaro2017merra2} supplies hourly aerosol
species, from which a surface \pmtf{} estimate is reconstructed (Section~\ref{sec:align}), and total
AOD; six days of September 2025 are missing in the Earth Engine copy.

Static features describe each station's surroundings: elevation from SRTM \cite{farr2007srtm},
land-cover shares (tree, crop, built-up, water, grass and shrub) within 5\,km from ESA WorldCover
2021 \cite{zanaga2022worldcover}, population within 5\,km from WorldPop \cite{worldpop} for the
year of each observation, and distance to the coast.

\section{Exploratory analysis}

\subsection{Satellite availability and cloud}

Satellite availability follows the monsoon (Figure~\ref{fig:sat_month}). It is high from January
to April, falls to a deep minimum in July and recovers partly from October. In July MAIAC AOD at
the station pixel exists on 0--5\,\% of station-days in every state except Tamil Nadu (13\,\%),
whose east coast receives less south-west monsoon rain; Tamil Nadu has its own dip in November,
during the north-east monsoon.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{eda_sat_availability_by_month}
\caption{Share of station-days with a satellite value by calendar month and state, 2021--2025, for NO\textsubscript{2} and MAIAC AOD at the station pixel and within 10\,km. Shading marks the south-west monsoon.}
\label{fig:sat_month}
\end{figure}

The mechanism is cloud (Figure~\ref{fig:aod_cloud}). AOD at the station pixel is available on
70\,\% of clear days (TROPOMI cloud fraction below 0.1) and on 1\,\% of overcast days (0.9 and
above). TROPOMI NO\textsubscript{2} within 10\,km falls from 99\,\% to about 2\,\%. The UV aerosol index is
retrieved under cloud and is always present.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{eda_aod_vs_cloud}
\caption{Satellite availability against TROPOMI cloud fraction (10\,km mean), 2021--2025.}
\label{fig:aod_cloud}
\end{figure}

Table~\ref{tab:feasibility} shows the consequence for the \pmtf{} record. Of the 114,191 valid
station-days (before quality control), only a third have AOD at the station pixel, and in the Kerala
south-west monsoon roughly one in eight. The 10\,km buffer roughly triples the monsoon coverage but
still leaves most monsoon days without AOD. The gap is even larger in Karnataka (3.0\,\% at the
pixel, 15.9\,\% within 10\,km) and Telangana (6.5\,\%, 21.0\,\%) during the south-west monsoon.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\pmtf{}}{PM2.5} patterns}

\pmtf{} is lowest in the south-west monsoon in every state (Table~\ref{tab:season},
Figure~\ref{fig:pm_month}), when rain washes particles out of the air. Kerala has the flattest
seasonal cycle: its monsoon median stays near 24\ugm{}, while Karnataka's falls to 16\ugm{}. The
months with the fewest satellite observations are therefore also the months with the smallest
\pmtf{} signal, except in Kerala. Station medians range from 15.0 to 48.9\ugm{}, with a median of
26.3\ugm{}.

\subsection{Correlations}

Across all days, the strongest single predictors of daily \pmtf{} (Spearman $\rho$) are MERRA-2
\pmtf{} (0.53), TROPOMI CO (0.46), precipitation ($-0.42$), MERRA-2 AOD (0.37), NO\textsubscript{2}
(0.35) and relative humidity ($-0.35$). MAIAC AOD is weaker (0.22 at the pixel, 0.25 within 10\,km).
Much of this is the shared seasonal cycle: correlations within a season are lower. In Kerala from
June to September no satellite or reanalysis aerosol variable correlates with daily \pmtf{} at
$|\rho| > 0.10$.

Neighbouring stations agree only moderately (Figure~\ref{fig:station_corr}). For 5,594 station
pairs with at least 180 shared days, the median correlation of daily \pmtf{} is 0.50 within 10\,km,
0.42--0.48 between 10 and 200\,km, 0.41 at 200--400\,km and 0.35--0.38 beyond 400\,km. Strong local variation of this kind means that
estimates for a station the model has not seen will be much less accurate than estimates for
known stations.
