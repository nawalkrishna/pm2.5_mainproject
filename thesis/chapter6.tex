\chapter{Conclusion}

This study measured how cloud cover and the monsoon limit satellite-based estimation of daily
ground-level \pmtf{} in South India, using quality-controlled data from 113 stations over 2021--2025
together with TROPOMI, MAIAC, ERA5/ERA5-Land, MERRA-2 and static land-surface data.

Cloud and the monsoon act mainly by removing satellite coverage. Even within 10\,km of the station,
MAIAC AOD is available on only 9\,\% of station-days with a cloud fraction above 0.8 and on 37\,\% of
Kerala's south-west monsoon days. Where AOD is available, the satellite's advantage does not shrink
with cloud; there is simply much less of it.

Where satellite data exist, and MERRA-2 and meteorology are already in the model, they add only a
small amount of skill at unmonitored locations: $+0.020$ \rsq{} $[+0.009, +0.031]$ in
leave-one-station-out validation, and $+0.049$ $[+0.018, +0.096]$ in the south-west monsoon. In the
spatially blocked split the gain is $+0.004$ to $+0.023$ across processing variants, often with an
interval that includes zero. The satellite inputs add nothing for gap filling at a monitored
station. Tier B, which uses no satellite data, therefore recovers essentially all of the two-tier
skill. For an unmonitored location that skill is modest (LOSO \rsq{} 0.32; 0.35 with linear
regression), and in Kerala's monsoon
no model beats a monthly climatology without the station's own measurements.

For hourly forecasting at existing stations, a Random Forest on issue-time features is the best
model, or tied for best, at every horizon from 1 to 72 hours (\rsq{} 0.813 at 1 hour, 0.430 at 24 hours and 0.321 at
72 hours). An LSTM and a graph network are significantly below it in every seed, and satellite
inputs again add nothing.

Together these results answer the research question: in South India the monsoon makes the
satellite-free tier the core of any daily \pmtf{} system, and the value of satellite data lies in
small, location-specific gains rather than in the bulk of the skill.

\section{Future work}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Test whether the small spatial satellite gain survives stronger models, such as tuned
        gradient boosting with nested validation, fitted with several random seeds.
  \item Add spatial predictors for unmonitored sites, such as distance-weighted \pmtf{} from other
        stations on the same day, with care to avoid leakage in leave-one-station-out validation.
  \item Extract the inputs on a regular 1\,km grid and produce daily \pmtf{} maps for the region.
  \item Treat the Kerala monsoon separately, for example with long-range transport and rainfall
        washout features or a coarser target scale.
  \item For forecasting, replace reanalysis with archived real-time weather forecasts, so that the
        evaluation matches operational conditions.
\end{enumerate}
